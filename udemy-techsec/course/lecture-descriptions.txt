Understand how the Internet works—from packets and protocols to encryption and HTTPS security

Course Description

This course offers a clear and practical introduction to how the Internet works, guiding you from the fundamental mechanics of data transmission all the way to modern security techniques that protect everyday online communication.

We begin by building an intuitive understanding of networking from the ground up. You will learn how data is broken into packets, how those packets travel across shared infrastructure, and how routing decisions are made using IP addresses. From there, we explore how systems like DNS make the Internet usable for humans by translating names into network locations.

Next, the course explains how reliable communication is achieved over an inherently unreliable network. You will see how TCP ensures that data arrives correctly, in order, and at the right speed, forming the foundation for everything from email to streaming video. We then move up to the application layer, where protocols like HTTP power the web through simple but powerful request-response interactions.

In the second half of the course, we shift focus to security. You will learn how encryption protects confidentiality, how hashing ensures data integrity, and how public key cryptography solves the challenge of secure communication between strangers. Finally, we examine how digital certificates and certificate authorities establish trust, enabling secure connections like HTTPS.

Throughout the course, the emphasis is on understanding rather than memorization. By the end, you will have a strong mental model of how the Internet operates and how its core technologies fit together in a coherent, real-world system.

Who This Course Is For

This course is designed for learners who want a clear, conceptual understanding of how the Internet works without needing advanced mathematics or prior programming experience. It is especially valuable for students, educators, and professionals who use technology every day but want to better understand what happens “under the hood.”

If you are beginning your journey in computing, this course will give you a strong mental model of networking and security concepts that are foundational to fields like web development, cybersecurity, and IT. If you already have some technical experience, it will help you connect the pieces—from IP and TCP to HTTP and encryption—into a coherent, end-to-end picture.

This course is also a good fit for instructors looking for approachable material to support teaching, or for curious learners who enjoy understanding the deeper structure behind everyday technologies like the web, secure connections, and online communication.

Title: IP Addresses and the Internet Layer Explained
Description:
Understand how the Internet Protocol routes packets across networks using IP addresses. This lecture covers address structure, network prefixes, and how routers efficiently forward data across multiple hops to reach the correct destination computer.

CC-03-DNS.txt

Title: DNS: Turning Names into Network Addresses
Description:
Explore how the Domain Name System translates human-friendly names into IP addresses. Learn how DNS works as a distributed, cached system that allows users to navigate the Internet without memorizing numeric addresses.

CC-04-TCP.txt

Title: TCP: Reliable Data Delivery Over the Internet
Description:
Discover how TCP ensures reliable communication over an unreliable network. This lecture explains acknowledgements, retransmission, and flow control, showing how TCP guarantees ordered, complete data delivery across the Internet.

Title: TCP Streams: Building Reliable End-to-End Connections
Description:
Learn how TCP transforms unreliable packet delivery into a dependable data stream. This lecture shows how buffering, sequencing, and acknowledgements create the illusion of a continuous, reliable connection between applications.

2013-04-Metcalfe-Copyright-IEEE.pdf

Title: Bob Metcalfe: The Birth of Ethernet

Description:
In this interview, Bob Metcalfe recounts the creation of Ethernet at Xerox PARC and the challenges of networking early personal computers. He explains how the need to connect high-speed printers and multiple machines led to a shared cable design with collision detection and randomized retransmission. Bob Metcalfe also reflects on the evolution of Ethernet standards and how his early work helped shape the foundation of modern local area networks and today’s high-speed connectivity.

2012-05-Van-Jacobson-Copyright-IEEE.pdf

Title: Van Jacobson: Scaling the Early Internet

Description:
In this interview, Van Jacobson explains the challenges of scaling TCP/IP during the early days of NSFNet. He describes how congestion collapse nearly brought the network to a halt and how the development of the slow-start algorithm helped stabilize and grow the modern Internet. Van Jacobson provides a rare, firsthand look at the engineering decisions that made large-scale networking possible and how collaboration across the community led to rapid solutions.

Description:
In this interview, Vint Cerf—one of the “fathers of the Internet”—shares the origins of packet switching and the development of TCP/IP. He traces the evolution from early research in distributed communication to the creation of ARPANET and the eventual rise of the Internet. Vint Cerf highlights the importance of open protocols, collaboration, and long-term research in building the global network we rely on today.

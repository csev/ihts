When we started this course,
the Internet was just an idea.

A small network,
a few experiments,
and a big question:

Could we connect computers across distance?

We followed that journey—
from ARPANET
to NSFNET,
to the global Internet we rely on today.

We saw how the Web made it usable,
how open standards made it scalable,
and how communities made it thrive.

But here’s the thing.

This course focused on what happened.

The next question is:

How does it actually work?

When you click a link,
what really happens?

How does data move across networks?
How do browsers and servers communicate?
Why do some systems scale
while others fail?

And maybe most importantly:

Where are the weak points?

Because the same openness
that made the Internet powerful
also makes it vulnerable.

That’s where the next course comes in:

Internet Technology and Security.

In that course,
we go under the hood.

We look at protocols like HTTP, DNS, and TCP/IP.
We explore how systems are built—
and how they break.

We talk about security—
not as fear,
but as understanding.

Because once you understand how things work,
you start to see:

Why attacks succeed.
How systems defend themselves.
And how to think like an engineer
instead of just a user.

If this course gave you the story,
the next course gives you the x-ray vision.

So if you’re ready to go deeper,
I’ll see you in
Internet Technology and Security.

Thanks for being part of this journey.

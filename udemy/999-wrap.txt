








When we started this course,
we looked at the pre Internet
and its earliest formative days.

You have met influential
researtchers, looked at 
a few experimental machines,
and together asked a simple
question:

Could computers talk to each other?

From those early moments—
ARPANET, packet switching,
and the first fragile connections—
we followed a path that
changed the world.

We saw how the network grew
from a research project
into a global infrastructure.

We talked about the transition
from ARPANET to NSFNET,
and how policy, funding,
and collaboration shaped its growth.

We explored the invention of the Web,
and how a simple idea—
linking information—
using a web "browser" - 
made the Internet usable for everyone.

And along the way,
we met the people who made it happen.

Not just brilliant individuals,
but communities—
open, collaborative,
and often driven by curiosity more than profit.

If there’s one takeaway from this course,
it’s this:

The Internet was not inevitable.

It is the result of choices—
technical choices,
policy decisions,
and human values.

And those choices continue today.

The Internet you use now
is still evolving.

So as you move forward,
don’t just use it—
understand it.

Because once you understand it,
you see it differently.

You see the layers,
the tradeoffs,
and the opportunities.

Thanks for spending this time with me.
It’s been a pleasure.












Hello and welcome to my 
Internet History Course

My name is Charles Severance,
and I’m a faculty member
at the University of Michigan
School of Information.

I’ve been working on this material
for nearly 30 years,

and for me it really goes back
to the mid-1990s

when the Internet
was just getting started.

At that time,
I had the chance to work
with some friends
to produce a television show
about the Internet.

We didn’t know
what it would become,

so we went around filming people,
capturing ideas,

and collecting what turned into
an informal oral history
of the early Internet.

Around the same time,
I was also experimenting
with teaching online.

In fact,
I was one of the first professors
to stream a for-credit semester-long
class entirely online.

It was so early
that I couldn’t even stream video—

just audio and slides.

Over the years,
I kept collecting stories,
building teaching tools,

and interviewing people
who helped create the Internet.

All of that came together
in the original version
of this course in 2013.

This latest version
is an updated and refined version.

The idea behind this course
is simple:

You can’t really understand technology
without understanding its history.

Technology evolves over time,
shaped by people,

constraints,

and decisions
that didn’t always seem important
at the moment.

This course grew out of a Python class
I taught for many years in the
mid 2000's,

Right after the midterm exam,
I would give students
a break from programming

and step back
to give their learning a larger
context.

So that’s the backstory.

Now let’s get started.

And we’ll begin
at what I think of 
as the beginning—

Code breaking at Bletchley Park
in the 1940's just north of
London, England.
